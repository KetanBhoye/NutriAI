Data sources

NutriAI · Last updated 23 August 2026

NutriAI's nutrition figures come from other people's work. This page credits it, states the licence each source is used under, and says what we changed. Every food in the app records which source row it came from, so any number can be traced back.

Indian dishes — Indian Nutrient Databank

The Indian dishes in NutriAI's shared library come from the Indian Nutrient Databank, which computes nutrient values for recipes commonly eaten in India using India's own food composition tables.

Anuvaad / Jaacks Lab. Indian Nutrient Databank (INDB), version 2024.11. anuvaad.org.in/indb

INDB is built on:

Longvah, T., Ananthan, R., Bhaskarachary, K., & Venkaiah, K. (2017). Indian Food Composition Tables 2017. National Institute of Nutrition, Indian Council of Medical Research, Hyderabad, India.

Gopalan, C., Rama Sastri, B. V., & Balasubramanian, S. C. (2004). Nutritive Value of Indian Foods. National Institute of Nutrition, Indian Council of Medical Research, Hyderabad, India.

Public Health England (2021). McCance and Widdowson's The Composition of Foods Integrated Dataset. Open Government Licence v3.0. · U.S. Department of Agriculture, FoodData Central (public domain), for ingredients absent from the Indian tables.

What we changed. Values are stored per gram rather than per 100 g, and portion weights are derived from the published per-serving figures. We exclude some rows — mainly deep-fried dishes whose recipes count all of the frying oil into the dish, and soups whose stated energy does not match their stated macros. Those exclusions are ours, not the databank's.

Packaged products — Open Food Facts

Barcode scanning and product search use Open Food Facts, a crowd-sourced database of packaged foods.

Open Food Facts contributors. Open Food Facts. world.openfoodfacts.org

Product data is used under the Open Database Licence (ODbL) v1.0. Because it is crowd-sourced it contains real errors, so NutriAI warns you when a product's own numbers do not add up.

Generic foods — USDA FoodData Central

U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research Service. FoodData Central. fdc.nal.usda.gov · Public domain (CC0 1.0).

Anything you add yourself

Foods you log by hand, by photo or by talking to the coach are yours. They stay in your own library unless several unrelated people log the same everyday food, which is the only way an entry becomes shared — see the Privacy Policy.

Something credited wrongly, or a source that would like different wording? Write to hello@nutriai.app and it will be corrected.
